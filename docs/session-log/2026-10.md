Writing a refunds page out of what the code does, rather than out of what we
intended, is the only version of it that cannot quietly become false.

### Prices are computed, not typed

Every figure comes from `TRIP_PASS_PRICE_ILS`, `TRIP_PASS_DAYS` and the check's
own `priceLabel()`. This is the same page that would otherwise have gone stale

A refund for a pass bought by mistake and never used. The page invites the buyer
to write in and says we will look at it case by case, which is true and is what
the subscription section already does for the premium-to-pro switch. Inventing a
blanket refund right would have been a commitment nobody made, and the honest
alternative - a `Gap` box on a live consumer page - reads worse than an
invitation to make contact.

## 2026-10-03 (e) - the privacy clause that had to exist before a trip could go public

Entry (d) left one blocking item: `/privacy` promised nothing about publishing a
user's own content to the open web, and the publish feature cannot honestly go
live without it. Netanel ran `sql/supabase-public-trips.sql` and asked for the
clause, so this is that.

**New section 5, "פרסום טיול באתר - רק אם בחרתם בכך".** Sections 5-11 were
renumbered to 6-12 and the two internal cross-references to the rights section
("בסעיף 7" -> "בסעיף 8") moved with them. A policy whose numbering drifts from its
own references is worse than one with a section in an odd place, so the
renumbering was done rather than appending at the end.

### Why it is a top-level section rather than a line in section 4

Section 4 is "who the data reaches", and it is about **processors** - Supabase,
Vercel, Anthropic - each bound by a contract and each reversible. Publishing is
categorically different: it is the open web, and it is the only place on this site
where a user's own content becomes public. It earns its own section.

### What the clause commits to, and why each line is safe to promise

Every claim maps to something enforced in `lib/trip/publicTrip.ts` and asserted
by `publicTrip.test.ts`, which checks **by bytes** that none of these survives:
the trip name, the per-day notes, the pins (the hotel, and sometimes a home
address), the exact dates, the preferences (kashrut and Shabbat among them), what
friends wrote or voted, the owner's identity, and any stop that is not a catalog
place. The copy and the module cannot drift apart silently - if they ever
disagree, the module is right and the text is the bug, and that is written into
the comment above the section.

Three things the clause says that the code does and a reader would not guess:

- **Login is required to publish**, and the reason given is the user's, not ours:
  it is what lets them remove the page later from any device.
- **The published copy is a snapshot, not a live window.** Later edits do not
  publish themselves. So the consent given was to the itinerary as it stood at
  that moment, which is the honest reading of what they agreed to.
- **Removal is immediate and answers 410**, the snapshot is deleted in the same
  write, and only a tombstone remains so the URL can keep answering 410.

### The paragraph that admits a limit

"כל עוד העמוד היה פומבי, ייתכן שגורמים חיצוניים - מנועי חיפוש, ארכיונים, או מי
שפשוט שמר צילום מסך - החזיקו עותק." Our removal is instant; what a third party
cached is not ours to delete. A privacy policy that implies otherwise is making a
promise it cannot keep, and this is the one irreversible edge of the whole
feature.

## 2026-10-03 (d) - travellers can publish a trip, and it is a reconstruction rather than a copy

The second half of the Phase 4 work: `/trips/<slug>`, an owner-published,
indexable page built from a trip. **Not shipped to production as a working
feature yet** - `sql/supabase-public-trips.sql` has to be run first; see the end.

### The design decision the whole thing rests on

The obvious implementation is "copy the trip, delete the private fields". That is
a denylist, and a denylist silently leaks whatever gets added to `Trip` next -
which has already happened twice in this codebase's life (pins, then preferences).

So `lib/trip/publicTrip.ts` builds the public version **out of catalog ids**:
city slugs, place ids validated against the catalog, and a month. Nothing else is
carried, so there is no field to forget. Names, descriptions, photos, coordinates,
day summaries and the spend are all looked up from the catalog at render time.

The test asserts this by bytes, not by shape: a trip is loaded with a surname in
the title, a hotel pin with a street address, notes containing a confirmation
number and two friends' names, kashrut and Shabbat preferences and exact dates,
and the serialised snapshot is searched for each string. `assert(snapshot.pins ===
undefined)` would have passed on the day somebody adds `Trip.homeAddress`.

**Dates become a month.** "15-29 August" is a burglary window; "August" is the
most useful fact a reader wants. The year is dropped too - the season repeats, and
a year makes a good itinerary look stale in eighteen months.

### Opt-in and auto-publish were contradictory in the brief

Item 1 asked for explicit consent, item 4 for auto-publishing above 8 stops. The
reading applied, and it is in the code as two separate functions: **the owner
always opts in**, and the stop count decides only whether the resulting page is
`noindex` and whether it enters the sitemap. A thin trip still publishes - the
owner asked - and the panel says so afterwards as a fact rather than in front as
a hurdle.

### Five things that only showed up by running it

1. **410 cannot come from a page.** Next 16 has `notFound()`, `forbidden()` and
   `unauthorized()`, and no `gone()`. The withdrawn-page status therefore comes
   from a new `src/middleware.ts`, matched to `/trips/` only, reading a
   catalog-free status module (the full `publicTrips.ts` imports 4MB of catalog,
   which must never enter an Edge bundle). It **fails open**: every error returns
   'unknown' and the request falls through, because a live page briefly answering
   410 is unrecoverable while the reverse is not.
2. **`/destinations/<slug>` is SSG.** A server-rendered "travellers' trips" block
   there would have been frozen at build time - a trip published on Tuesday would
   appear at the next deploy. The block is now a client component over
   `/api/trips/city`, so all 170 catalog pages stay fully static and render
   byte-identically until a city actually has a published trip.
3. **`export const revalidate` prerenders at build.** `/sitemap-trips.xml` with
   `revalidate = 3600` was generated during the build, with an empty database, and
   served an empty sitemap. Now `force-dynamic` with the freshness in
   `Cache-Control` - same request volume, no empty-at-deploy window.
4. **The site-wide CSP blocked the embed entirely.** `next.config.ts` applies
   `frame-ancestors 'self'` and `x-frame-options: SAMEORIGIN` to `/:path*`, which
   replaced the embed route's own header - a blogger's iframe would have rendered
   blank with the only evidence a console message on *their* site. The blanket
   rule is now `/((?!embed/).*)` with a separate, **narrower** policy for the
   embed (`default-src 'none'`, one same-origin script) whose only wider grant is
   `frame-ancestors *`. `x-frame-options` is absent rather than permissive,
   because the header has no "allow any site" value.
5. **The embed cannot be a page at all.** A page inherits the root layout, i.e.
   the entire site, inside somebody's article. It is a Route Handler returning a
   standalone 2.4KB document: no React, no hydration, no client bundle.

### Guards that caught real defects, again

- The **AA contrast** test rejected `text-night/60` (4.18:1); the floor is `/65`.
- The **URL-in-attribute** guard caught the embed route and, more importantly,
  `EmbedSnippet` - which looks like a display string and is in fact HTML about to
  be pasted onto a domain we do not control. Both now go through `safeUrlAttr`.
- The **backup-table** test refused a new table nobody had decided about.
  `public_trips` is backed up: the rows hold no personal data, but they are
  irreplaceable - the stripped snapshot is the only copy, and it cannot be
  re-derived without asking the owner to consent a second time.
- `englishComments` and the **Hebrew-prefix** rule fired again. Note the prefix
  guard only sees the template-literal form, so JSX (`ב{cityName}`) is its blind
  spot - that instance was found by eye and fixed with `inHe`.

### Deliberately not done

- **Cost is shown in the source's own currency, not converted to shekels.** The
  brief asked for ILS. There is no exchange rate anywhere in this codebase, and
  putting an invented one on an indexable page is precisely what hard rule 2
  forbids. Domestic destinations publish in ILS and therefore already read as
  shekels; Rome reads EUR 50-100.
- **No cost in the JSON-LD.** `estimatedCost` has a domain of `HowTo` and is
  invalid on a `TouristTrip`; `offers` would validate and would claim the trip is
  for sale. The node is validated against the real schema.org vocabulary by
  `scripts/validate-jsonld.mjs`, which now covers it (9 nodes, all valid).
- **`/privacy` has not been updated**, and it must be before this goes live.

### Verified end to end

Against a production build, with a stand-in Supabase on localhost implementing
only the endpoints this feature touches - **deliberately not the real project's
database, because creating tables there is Netanel's call, not a side effect of a
session**. Publish -> 200 page, 8 catalog stops (the 9th, a lodging pin, dropped),
indexable, in the sitemap, in the city block, embeddable. Unpublish -> **410**,
out of the sitemap, out of the city block, embed 410 too. The ten private strings
listed above appear **zero** times in either public document.

### Two traps this walked into and out of

- **`position: fixed` inside `.rise-in`.** `SharedTripView`'s root carries
  `.rise-in`, which keeps its final transform forever, and a transform creates a
  containing block - so a fixed bar nested in it is measured against the article,
  not the viewport. This is the documented trap that caught `TripWorkspace`'s
  chat bar. Same fix as that one: the bar is a **sibling** of `.rise-in` inside a
  fragment, with `clears-chat-bar` reserving its height. Measured afterwards: the
  only fixed element in that corner is the accessibility button (44px at
  x=330..374), and the bar stops at x=310 - a 20px gap, and below its z-index.
- **Owner detection without `set-state-in-effect`.** The lint rule is an error in
  this repo, so ownership reads through `useSyncExternalStore` with a no-op
  subscribe: ownership cannot change while the page is open. The server snapshot
  is `false` on purpose - the first paint is the visitor's view, because an owner
  seeing a stranger's CTA for one frame is harmless and the reverse would hide
  the invitation from every real visitor.

### The link preview, and the thing it was leaking

`sharePreview()` is now the single source for both the `og:title`/`og:description`
and the generated card, which were deriving days/cities/stops separately.

**The card used to lead with the trip's name, and that name is the owner's free
text.** A share link goes to people the owner chose; its WhatsApp card gets
forwarded onward to people they did not. The title is now generated from the
itinerary - length, city, stop count, the places worth naming - which is both
safer and a better advert than somebody's private trip name. Verified on a real
link: the name appears twice in the document body and **zero times in `<head>`**.

Two Hebrew bugs surfaced on the way, both caught by guards that already existed:

- The two-city label was built as "A and B". The conjunction is a one-letter
  prefix, so it hits the vav-doubling rule - but `hePrefix` is built for the
  prepositional prefixes, and applying it to the conjunction before a
  vav-initial name yields **three** consecutive vavs ("Venice" is exactly this
  case). Gluing the letter on by hand is what `hebrewPrefixSites.test.ts`
  rejects, and it rejected the line. Rather than invent a spelling rule, the
  label now names one city and counts the rest - which is also the format the
  brief asked for.
- `englishComments.test.ts` caught Hebrew in the new comments, twice.

### Analytics: two new events, not four

The brief asked for `share_view`, `share_cta_click`, `share_duplicate` and
`trip_created_from_share`. Only the middle two were added. `shared_trip_opened`
and `shared_trip_adopted` already measure a shared view and a share that led to a
trip, are already in the reports, and a second name for the same moment produces
two charts that disagree by a few percent forever. Worth knowing: `shared_adopt`
only fires for a browser that held **no** trips on arrival, so `share_duplicate`
is also the only signal when an existing planner copies somebody's trip.

Attribution (`lib/shareAttribution.ts`) is a separate key from the existing share
marker on purpose - that one stores a day and only for brand-new browsers,
because it is a counter. This one records **which** link, on the trip itself
(`Trip.source`), first-link-wins, consumed once, expiring after 7 days. It is
never encoded into a share link, so a trip's origin does not travel to the next
recipient and cannot form a chain of who sent what to whom.

Against a production build over CDP, on a real inline share link (4 days, Rome +

## 2026-10-03 - the four seconds of beige screen, and the copy that read like a model

**The complaint was "the site takes a few seconds to load with a beige screen", and the
cause was not what anyone would have guessed.** The assumption behind the request was
that something is being fetched and the screen waits for it, so the fix would be
skeleton loaders. It was not. The HTML was complete and correct in **300ms**, the CSS
was done at 915ms, the DOM was interactive at 851ms - and the browser then painted
nothing at all for several more seconds.

### Measuring it honestly, which took two false starts

- The first measurement said FCP was 8,616ms. It was an artifact: the browser-automation
  extension leaves its tab **backgrounded**, and Chrome does not paint a hidden tab.
  `document.visibilityState` was `hidden`. Any paint timing collected that way is
  meaningless.
- The second attempt took screenshots to see the gap. That is also invalid:
  `Page.captureScreenshot` **forces a paint**, so it moves the number it is measuring.
  It did prove the content was ready early - a forced paint at 300ms showed the full,
  correct homepage.
- What worked: headless Edge over CDP (the recipe already in the project's memory),
  which paints normally, with `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate` 4x and a slow-4G profile
  - a mid-range phone. Plus a `longtask` PerformanceObserver installed via
  `Page.addScriptToEvaluateOnNewDocument`, i.e. before navigation, so nothing is missed.

That showed one **3,055ms main-thread task starting the instant HTML parsing ended**.
Isolating it by page: `/about` blocked 67ms before paint, `/chat` 675ms, the homepage
**3,904ms**. So it was not the shared layout and not HTML size (`/countries` ships 662KB
of HTML and blocks 90ms).

A CPU profile named it: **1,511ms of self time in gtag.js**. The A/B, blocking
googletagmanager at the network layer and changing nothing else:

| | first contentful paint | blocked before paint |
|---|---|---|
| gtag via `afterInteractive` | 6,700-8,732ms | 2,868-5,904ms |
| gtag blocked | 2,896-2,912ms | 1,343-1,588ms |

**Analytics was costing about four seconds of blank cream screen.**

### Why the old comment was wrong, and what replaced it

`Analytics.tsx` asserted that `afterInteractive` "blocks nothing: not the first paint,
not hydration." That was reasoning from the attribute, not from a measurement. `async`
means the script does not block the HTML *parser*; it does nothing to stop ~100KB of
third-party JavaScript from monopolising the main thread in the window where the browser
would otherwise have painted. On a fast desktop CPU the effect is invisible, which is
why it survived.

`lazyOnload` is still not the answer, for exactly the reason recorded when it was
removed: it injects the tag client-side after load, so the tag is **absent from the
server HTML** that Google's "we have not detected the tag" check reads. That was a real
problem and it was correctly fixed once already.

So the two concerns were separated, because they were never the same concern:

- **The URL stays in the server HTML**, as `<link rel="preload" as="script"
  fetchPriority="low">`. A scanner reading the markup finds the exact gtag URL. A
  preload does not execute - and executing, not downloading, was the expensive part.
- **Execution waits one frame past the first paint**, then an idle callback with a 2s
  cap so a busy tab still reports.
- **No hit is lost by arriving later.** The inline consent/init scripts still run at
  `afterInteractive` and queue onto `dataLayer`; gtag.js replays the queue when it
  loads. The loader also waits for the `gtag` the consent script defines, so the consent
  default cannot lose the race.

### On the skeleton loaders that were asked for

The site already had a complete skeleton system (`Skeleton.tsx` primitives,
`TripSkeleton`, `AccountSkeleton`, `TravelerSkeleton`, `JoinSkeleton`, a route-level
`/t/[code]/loading.tsx`, skeleton rows in site search), and it follows a documented
policy. **A skeleton could not have fixed this bug**: a skeleton is in the same HTML and
starved by the same main thread, so a browser that cannot paint the hero cannot paint a
placeholder for it either. Adding them to the homepage would have added DOM and JS and
made it slightly worse.

One real gap did exist and is fixed: **`AddDayPicker` told the traveller the catalog was
empty while it was still loading.** The city list is fetched on first open, so until it
arrived `filtered.length` was 0 and the component rendered its no-results message -
"אין עיר כזו בקטלוג" - about a city the user had not finished typing. "Empty" had three
meanings sharing one message; now loading draws skeleton rows, a failed fetch says so
(and is not cached, so re-opening retries), and only a real empty result keeps the
catalog line.

### The copy pass

The ask was "professional, but more human and less AI". The dominant tell was mechanical
rather than a matter of taste: **" - " used as a rhetorical pivot mid-sentence, 564 times
across 94 files**, with stacked qualifier clauses and a relentless rule-of-three. A
script found and counted them so the work was targeted rather than vibes-based; down to
**336**, and what remains is legal pages, the English model prompt and the admin
dashboard.

No factual claim changed - the hard-rule-2 caveats are all still present, just phrased
less like a brochure. Covered: homepage sections and hero, `/chat` and `/ask` landings,
the pricing page, trip screen and chat panel, shared-trip page, account/join/planner,
the SEO collection hubs and destination guides, the agent's own spoken caveats
(`priceGuard`, `chatGuards`), and the transactional emails - edited in
`scripts/build-emails.mjs`, which is the source of truth, then regenerated.

The agent's system prompt gained an explicit anti-AI-voice rule, since that is where
most of the Hebrew a user actually reads gets generated. Note this **invalidates the
Anthropic prompt cache once**, which is expected on any prompt change.

**Two stale-product bugs surfaced while reading the copy**, both from the retirement of
the monthly premium plan:

- The account menu offered "שדרוג לפרימיום · 19.90 ₪ לחודש" and linked to `/premium`,
  which does not sell that plan - a promised price absent from the page it links to. Now
  an unpriced link to the pricing page.
- The Pro card and a pricing FAQ described Pro **by comparison to Premium** ("exactly
  what Premium has, with more room", "Premium is your plan and Pro is a waste of money"),
  which is meaningless to a reader who cannot buy Premium. Both now stand on their own
  and compare Pro to the trip pass, which is what is actually sold.

### Deliberately not done

- **Legal pages** (privacy 66 dash-pivots, terms, refunds, cookies, accessibility,
  affiliate-disclosure) were left alone. A formal register is correct there and
  rewriting legal text risks changing meaning.
- **The catalog** (`src/data/destinations.ts`, 4.18MB) was not touched. Those are
  factual descriptions of real places; rewriting ~3,400 of them for tone is how
  inaccuracy gets introduced, which is the one thing hard rule 2 forbids.
- **The admin dashboard** is internal and not a visitor surface.

### Other things measured and found fine

- Every page is statically prerendered; TTFB on the live site is 290-460ms, gzipped.
- The 3.4MB catalog **is** in a browser-served chunk, but it is loaded lazily and only
  when site search is opened - a deliberate, documented optimisation that is working.
  It is not on the critical path.
- The remaining ~1.4s of pre-paint blocking is React hydration plus the Turbopack module
  loader, i.e. framework cost, not an application bug.
